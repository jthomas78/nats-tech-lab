Multi-region reference · demos/02-multi-region

Two Regions,
One NATS Supercluster

Ten patterns measured on a six-server lab — three in South Africa, three in Australia, joined by a gateway. Each card states the mechanism, what it costs, and the number the lab actually printed. Nothing here is from a blog post.

The question every card serves

A truck drives 12.5 km, once, in South Africa. Two regions are listening. What does the odometer say afterwards — and what happens to both regions when the link between them dies?

The rule that orders the deck

A gateway buys reach and sells you a shared namespace and a shared failure domain. Only the account is a wall. Everything else on these pages — subjects, placement, domains — is a convention, a location, or a name.

01
Namespace

One supercluster takes one JetStream domain

A gateway joins two clusters into one supercluster. A supercluster is one JetStream system, and a JetStream system has exactly one domain name. Every server carries the same one. Here it is lb.

Decision

Can a per-region domain (za, au) be used to keep two regions apart? No. It is not a legal configuration, and the servers do not tell you.

Mechanism
  • A domain names a JetStream system, not a region and not a tenant. The same name is required on every server of a cluster and of a supercluster.
  • The name may only change across a leaf-node link. A gateway is not a leaf link.
  • Setting domain also suppresses JetStream traffic on the system account — which is what silently breaks a supercluster that uses two names.
What it does give you
  • An address. $JS.lb.API.> lets a leaf node reach a named JetStream from outside it.
  • Clean addressing when you really do run separate JetStream systems joined by leaf nodes — see Card 09.
What it does NOT give you
  • Not a tenant boundary. That is the account — Card 05.
  • Not a location. That is placement — Card 03.
  • Not availability. A domain does not change the meta group — Card 08.
  • No error if you get it wrong. Two domains in one supercluster produce silence, not a message.
Measured, then reversed — 2026-09-09
  • This repo shipped za / au / hub domains, on the belief that a domain was a per-cluster boundary. It broke JetStream instead. The correction is dated in docs/Multi-Region-Plan.md at D9 and D11.
  • The lab now runs one domain, lb, on all six servers, and separates regions with accounts and placement.
Verdict

Treat domain as an address for leaf nodes, nothing else. If you are reaching for it to split two clusters that share a gateway, you are solving the wrong problem with the wrong tool.

boundary strength: none
Multi-region · nats-tech-labNamespace · Card 02 / 10
02
Namespace

The stream-name collision — error 10058

Inside one account, a stream name is unique across the whole supercluster. South Africa creates ODOMETER. Australia asks for ODOMETER and is refused. There is no second stream, and no warning that there is not.

Decision

Two regions both want a stream with the same, obvious, domain-correct name. Which axis do you spend to get it — the subject (Card 04), or the account (Card 05)?

Mechanism — measured by lab/00-the-problem.sh
  • The exact text: stream name already in use with a different configuration (10058).
  • Worse than the error: stream info from AU then succeeds and returns ZA's stream — same cluster, same created timestamp. AU has a working handle to somebody else's data.
  • One purge in ZA empties "both". They were always one object.
Why this is the dangerous one
  • The failure is at create time, once, in a script nobody reads twice. After that everything looks healthy.
  • Each region still runs its own writer and its own projector, and they now share one log. Every AU write and every AU read crosses the WAN. Nobody chose that, and nothing warns you.
  • A message count cannot show this, and neither can a business total — the total is right. Only stream info can: ask which cluster holds it. See Card 05.
  • If ZA goes off the air, AU loses the stream — because AU never had one.
What does not fix it
  • A domain. Card 01.
  • Placement. --cluster au chooses where a stream lives. It cannot make a second stream with a name the account has already used.
  • Bigger clusters, more replicas, a hub. None of these touch the namespace.
Verdict

A shared account across a gateway is a shared name space, a shared log and a shared bug. Discover it on day one with a deliberate collision test, not in month six with a wrong invoice.

detection: create-time only
Multi-region · nats-tech-labLocation · Card 03 / 10
03
Location

Placement — where a stream physically lives

In a supercluster, JetStream will otherwise choose for you. --cluster za pins the stream and every one of its replicas to the South African servers. It is the right tool for where, and it is not a boundary.

Decision

Where must this data sit — for latency, for data residency, for the blast radius of one region failing?

Mechanism
  • A stream's replicas never span clusters. Placement is not "prefer" — it is the fence the Raft group is built inside. Asking for more replicas than one cluster holds fails with no suitable peers for placement (10005), even when the supercluster has plenty of servers.
  • Omitting --cluster does not spread a stream. It lets the meta leader pick one cluster, possibly the far one.
  • Check it, do not assume it: nats stream info <name> --json | jq .cluster.name. The lab's lab/streams.sh prints this for all six contexts.
Pros
  • Real and physical. Data residency and read latency are decided here, not by naming.
  • Cheap — one flag, per stream, no trust-chain change.
  • Makes a region's failure legible: you know exactly which streams went with it.
Cons
  • Not a security or correctness boundary. Anyone in the account can read a ZA-placed stream from AU.
  • Does not solve the name collision — Card 02.
  • Silent by omission: forget the flag and placement happens anyway, somewhere.
  • Caps your replica count at the size of one cluster.
Say it once, clearly
  • Domain = which JetStream system. Placement = which cluster. Account = who may see it. Three different words, three different jobs, and only the third one is a wall.
Verdict

Set --cluster explicitly on every stream in a supercluster. It costs one flag and it removes an entire class of "why is our data in Sydney" question.

boundary strength: none · location: exact

04
Separation, by convention

A region token in the subject

Keep one account. Put the region in the subject, so the two streams no longer overlap: ZA filters evt.za.>, AU filters evt.au.>. It works. It is a naming convention, not a boundary.

Decision

Are you willing to pay for separation in subjects and stream names — every publisher, every consumer, every filter — to avoid changing the trust chain?

Mechanism — measured by lab/01-option-1-subjects.sh
  • The region token is paid TWICE. Once in the subject, and once in the stream name — because Card 02 still applies inside one account.
  • The message did cross the gateway. Nothing blocked it. AU simply had no filter that matched and threw it away.
  • What physically held the two streams apart was --cluster, not the subject.
Pros
  • No trust-chain change. No new accounts, no new credentials, no re-issue.
  • Gives AU a real local stream today, with a filter edit and a stream rename.
  • Easy to reason about in a small system with few publishers.
Cons
  • A typo misroutes, silently. Measured: a client on AU publishing evt.za.trip stored the message in ZA's stream, across the WAN; AU's stayed at 0. Not two copies — one copy in the wrong region, with no error anywhere. (A typo in a filter cannot happen: inside one account JetStream refuses overlapping subjects, 10065.)
  • Spends two of this repo's settled design rules: region is not a subject token, and streams are named for the domain, not the deployment.
  • Touches every publisher, consumer and filter — a wide, boring migration.
  • Still leaves two write sides in one account, with no story for merging them.
Verdict

It gives each region its own stream and fixes nothing else. Reasonable as a stop-gap while accounts are being minted; a poor destination, because its safety depends on everybody spelling things correctly forever.

boundary strength: convention
Multi-region · nats-tech-labSeparation, hard · Card 05 / 10
05
Separation, server-enforced

One account per region — the only real wall

Give South Africa LINEBOOKER_ZA and Australia LINEBOOKER_AU. Both may now hold a stream called ODOMETER, because they are two namespaces. The server checks the account on every message. Nothing depends on a name being right.

Decision

Is a region a tenant boundary — data that must not leak, and that each region must own outright — or only a deployment detail?

Mechanism — measured by lab/03-odometer.sh, in kilometres
AccountsOdometer in ZAOdometer in AUWho owns AU's data
one LINEBOOKER, both regions12.5 km12.5 kmnobody — the SAME bucket, in za
LINEBOOKER_ZA + LINEBOOKER_AU12.5 km0 kmau, in its own local stream

Corrected 2026-09-11. Row one used to read 25 km / 25 km / 50 km and was called a cross-region double capture. It was wrong twice. The 25 came from defer sub.Unsubscribe() in odometer/main.go deleting the durable pull consumer, so every projector run replayed the stream (measured: four runs over a stream holding one message read 12.5, 25, 37.5, 50 — one region, one account, no gateway). The 50 came from adding ZA's number to AU's when both read the same bucket. Behind a gateway there is nothing to capture twice. Full write-up: gateway-double-capture-options-2.html.

  • Same subject, same code, same single publish. Only the account changed.
  • Row one is the trap: the number looks right. Both readings come out of one bucket, held in za. AU pays WAN latency on every read and goes dark for that stream if za is lost.
  • A gateway carries interest, and it never crosses an account. That is the whole guarantee.
  • The tenant axis in this repo is the account, so a region-per-account decision must be taken with the tenancy model open, not separately.
Pros
  • Checked by the server on every message. Not a convention, not a filter, not a review comment.
  • The obvious, domain-correct stream name works in both regions.
  • Subjects stay clean — no region token, so the settled subject taxonomy survives.
  • A leak becomes structurally impossible rather than unlikely.
Cons
  • Trust-chain work. New accounts, new credentials, new exports/imports for anything that genuinely must cross.
  • Cross-region sharing is now explicit — you must design it, per subject.
  • Two write sides remain two write sides. The account makes them honest, not merged.
  • It does not help availability at all. Card 08.
Verdict

The destination. It is the only shape where each region owns its own data rather than borrowing the other's over the WAN, and the only one that keeps the domain-correct stream name in both regions. “AU has no odometer at all once za is lost” is what pays for the migration.

boundary strength: hard
Multi-region · nats-tech-labDurability · Card 06 / 10
06
Durability

Replicas and quorum — 1, 3, 5

A three-server cluster is not three copies. A stream stores the number of copies you ask for, and the default is one. Ask for one, lose one server, and the stream is gone.

Decision

Per stream: how many server failures must this data survive? Every extra copy is real disk and real latency — Card 07.

Measured on a 3-server cluster by lab/02-replicas.sh
ReplicasQuorumSurvivesStop the leader and…
1—nothingstream is offline (10118)
321 servernew leader in ~1s, messages intact
532 serversrefused — no suitable peers for placement (10005)
  • Use odd numbers only — 1, 3, 5. An even count adds cost and buys no extra tolerance, because quorum is floor(n/2)+1. NATS caps a stream at 5.
  • R5 needs five servers in one cluster. It will not borrow peers from the other region — a stream's replicas never span clusters (Card 03). Six servers across two clusters still gives 10005.
  • Replication is per stream. Nothing is global, and the default is the unsafe one.
When R1 is honestly right
  • Data you can regenerate — a cache, a derived projection, a scratch stream.
  • Development and lab work, where a rebuild costs a command.
  • Very high write rates where the source of truth is elsewhere.
When R1 is a hidden outage
  • Anything a business decision reads. 10118 is not a slow response — it is no response, until that one server returns.
  • Anything you cannot replay from an upstream system.
  • KV buckets. A bucket is a stream, so it has replicas too — and stream ls hides it. Check with nats kv ls.
Verdict

R3 is the working default for anything that matters; R1 is a deliberate, documented exception. R5 only where a five-server cluster already exists and the data justifies the write cost.

default: 1 · use: 3

Durability, priced

What replication costs — the acknowledgement

R3 does not only slow the publish. Both kinds of acknowledgement are writes, so both slow down: the publisher's PubAck, and the consumer's Ack(), because a consumer's position is replicated too.

Decision

You are sizing a throughput budget. Do you cost the extra copies as disk, or as round-trips on both sides of the stream? It is both.

Measured with nats bench, one cluster, same hardware
PathR1R3Cost
Synchronous publish (wait for PubAck)4,272 msg/s · P50 212 µs2,761 msg/s · P50 331 µs~35% slower
Consumer fetch + Ack()196,864 msg/s · avg 30.71 µs121,532 msg/s · avg 36.24 µs~38% slower
  • The consumer's Ack() is not free bookkeeping. It moves the consumer's position, that position lives in a replicated Raft group, so it pays the same quorum round-trip.
  • Batch and AckAll where the domain allows it — the fix for ack cost is fewer acks, not fewer replicas.
  • These are lab numbers on one machine. The ratio is the transferable part, not the rate.
The trap that made this card necessary
  • nats pub is a core publish. It is fire-and-forget: no PubAck, no wait, and no error when JetStream cannot store the message. It prints "Published" during an outage and looks like success.
  • An early quorum test here was misread for exactly this reason. The truth only appeared in the message count: 1 during the outage, 2 after recovery.
  • Real code must use a JetStream publish and check the ack. In benchmarks, use nats bench --js with a synchronous publish, or you are timing the network card.
Verdict

Budget roughly a third less throughput for R3 on both the write and the ack path. If that is unaffordable for a stream, the honest answer is a different stream design — not R1 on data you cannot lose.

R1 → R3 ≈ −35% both ways

08
Availability

The shared failure domain — a WAN cut

A gateway makes all six servers one JetStream meta group. Majority of six is four. Cut the link 3 / 3 and neither side can elect a meta leader. Accounts do not change this, and neither does a domain.

Decision

What exactly stops when the WAN goes? The honest answer is much narrower than "both regions are down" — but it is not nothing.

Measured — AU stopped, ZA holding 3 of 6 peers, leader = NONE
Operation on the isolated sideWorks?Measured
Core NATS publish / subscribeyesmessage delivered
Read an existing streamyesmsgs=10
Write to an existing streamyes10 → 15, all acked
Create / delete / edit a stream or consumernoJetStream system temporarily unavailable (10008)
  • Existing streams keep serving because a stream's replicas all live inside one cluster (Card 03). That Raft group still has its own majority. Only the meta group is split.
  • So a WAN cut is a change freeze, not an outage. Plan it that way: no deploys that create streams or consumers, no scaling, no new tenants — but traffic keeps flowing.
  • Recovery needs no intervention. Restart the far side and the meta leader returns in seconds.
What the gateway really sold you
  • Reach, paid for with a shared control plane. The link between regions is a hard dependency for all stream management.
  • The failure is symmetric at 3+3 — nobody wins, so there is no "primary" to fail over to.
  • An account per region does not help. It is a data wall, not an availability wall.
Check it in one command
  • curl -s localhost:8621/jsz?meta=1 | jq '.meta_cluster' — gives leader and cluster_size.
  • leader: null with a healthy cluster_size is exactly this state.
  • Alert on it. It is invisible from the data plane until somebody deploys.
Verdict

Say "management freezes", never "the region is down". The difference decides whether you page somebody at 02:00 or write a ticket for the morning.

data plane: unaffected

09
Availability, by design

Escaping the shared failure domain

A 3 + 3 gateway is the worst arithmetic there is: an exact tie. Four shapes avoid it, and they are ordinary design choices, not tricks.

Decision

Must either side keep managing streams through a cut? If yes, the topology has to change — no setting will do it.

Four ways out, weakest first
ShapeArithmeticThrough a WAN cut
Uneven regions — 3 + 25 peers, majority 3the 3-side keeps its leader and stays fully writable; only the small side freezes
A third cluster (gateway hub)3 sites, one link losta majority survives; the usual production answer
Nothing at all — the data planeper-stream Raft, inside one clusteralready survives; see Card 08
Leaf nodes with their own domainseparate meta groupsgenuinely independent JetStream systems — nothing to split
  • No gateway at all is the fifth, degenerate case: two labs that cannot talk. Nothing collides and nothing survives a cut, because there is nothing to cut. It is not a boundary you designed.
  • A leaf link is the only place a JetStream domain may change (Card 01). This is what domains are actually for, and why they cannot help across a gateway.
Prefer a hub
  • Three sites, odd count, no tie. A lost link leaves a majority.
  • The hub carries no tenant data — it exists to hold a vote and to relay.
  • Named as future work in docs/Multi-Region-Plan.md; this lab has no hub.
Uneven sizes are a real answer
  • 3 + 2 is not "one region is worse". It is a decision about which side keeps working, made in advance and written down.
  • Say which side that is in the runbook, before the cut, not during it.
Verdict

Pick the arithmetic on purpose. Two equal regions joined by one gateway is the one shape that guarantees nobody can manage streams during a cut.

avoid: 3 + 3

Method

Measuring without fooling yourself

Three of the conclusions in this deck were wrong the first time. Each was wrong for the same reason: the test tool lied, and the result looked plausible. This card is the list of traps, so they are not walked into again.

Decision

Before you believe a distributed-systems measurement: what is the control probe that must still work? If it also failed, your harness is broken, not the system.

Trap 01 · Docker ate the ports
  • docker network disconnect lb-wan <container> on a running container also drops that container's published host ports.
  • Result: 4621 and 8621 go dead and everything looks catastrophic. Reported as "both regions died". It was a Docker artefact.
  • The giveaway: core NATS pub/sub also "failed" — impossible from a JetStream meta split. That is the control probe.
  • Do this instead: docker stop lab2-au-1 lab2-au-2 lab2-au-3. Ports stay up, majority is genuinely lost.
Trap 02 · "Published" is not "stored"
  • nats pub is a core publish. No PubAck, no wait, no error when JetStream cannot store it.
  • It printed "Published" all through an outage and made a broken quorum look healthy.
  • The giveaway: the message count. 1 during the outage, 2 after recovery.
  • Do this instead: assert on stream info counts, never on a publisher's own output.
Trap 03 · The shell did not split the flag
  • zsh does not word-split unquoted variables. C="--context lab2-za"; nats $C ls fails with unknown long flag '--context lab2-za'.
  • Cascades into confusing jq: parse error noise that hides the real cause.
  • Do this instead: use an array — C=(--context lab2-za) — or wrap the experiment in bash <<'EOF' … EOF.
The habits that caught all three
  • Always carry a control probe that must survive the fault. Core pub/sub here.
  • Assert on state, not on tool output. Counts, cluster.name, created timestamps, files on disk.
  • Prove the recovery too. A test that only shows the break can be a broken test.
  • Re-run the whole lab after every fix. All five stages, then lab/streams.sh and nats kv ls to prove nothing was left behind.
Verdict

A measurement you cannot explain the mechanism of is a rumour. Write the corrections down, dated, next to the claim they replace — this deck exists because three of them were not.

3 wrong results · all harness

Choosing

The whole deck, on one page

Two regions, one gateway. Three questions get asked in this order, and each has exactly one correct tool.

Question 1 — do the two regions collide?
TopologyStreams collide?Survives a WAN cut?

Q2 · Who may see it?
  • The account. Card 05.
  • Server-enforced on every message. The only wall in this deck.
  • A subject token (Card 04) is a convention and fails silently on a typo.
Q3 · Where does it live?
  • Placement. --cluster za|au. Card 03.
  • Set it explicitly, always. Replicas never span clusters.
  • domain is not this, and is not a boundary. Card 01.
Q4 · What survives?
  • Replicas. R3 default, R1 by exception. Card 06.
  • Costs ~35% on publish and on ack. Card 07.
  • A WAN cut freezes management only. Cards 08–09.
Six sentences to remember
  • A gateway makes one supercluster, and a supercluster is one JetStream system with one domain.
  • Inside one account, a stream name is unique across the whole supercluster.
  • Only the account is a wall. Subjects are conventions, placement is a location, a domain is an address.
  • The default is Replicas 1. Three servers is not three copies.
  • A WAN cut is a change freeze, not an outage — unless the regions are unevenly sized, in which case only the smaller one freezes.
  • A business total finds bugs a message count cannot — but a total that looks RIGHT can still be reading the wrong region's stream. Ask stream info which cluster holds it.
Verdict

Gateway plus one account per region plus explicit placement plus R3. Then decide, in advance and in writing, which side keeps managing streams when the link drops.
