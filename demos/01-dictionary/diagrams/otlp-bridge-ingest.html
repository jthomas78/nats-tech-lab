How the OTLP bridge ingests span data

The bridge is a second, independent consumer of a stream that already exists. No service imports an OTel library, and nothing in a service knows whether the bridge is running.

Consequences worth stating plainly

  • No service imports an OTel library. Not behind a build tag, not behind an env flag. Turning tracing export on or off is two docker-compose.yml services.
  • The bridge can be wrong without costing anything. If the OTLP mapping has a bug, the spans are still on TRACES and in KV — fix the bridge and replay. An in-process exporter would have already dropped them.
  • One copy of the OTLP mapping. natstrace is duplicated into all five services because each Dockerfile builds from its own directory; the bridge is the only component that knows OTLP exists, so that duplication never touches the export format.
  • Ordering is per-trace, not global. A single durable consumer feeds the projector, so a trace is assembled by one writer. The bridge makes no ordering assumption at all — OTLP spans carry their own parent pointers.
  • The account boundary is the real work. Everything else here is a consumer on a stream. The piece that needs care is the per-tenant obs.trace.> export and the allow_trace grant, because without it spans never leave the tenant account and the whole picture stops at the dashed line.
