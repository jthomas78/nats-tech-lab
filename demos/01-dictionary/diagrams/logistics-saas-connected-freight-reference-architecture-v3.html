Marketplace Tender-to-Settlement on NATS

This architecture shows how shippers, transporters, drivers and external systems collaborate through a governed logistics marketplace. Numbered flows follow the AWS reference-architecture convention; the services and guarantees remain the proposed NATS-based platform model.

1

Participants remain separate organisations. Shippers publish demand, transporters offer capacity, and drivers provide execution evidence through role-specific channels.

2

The shipper creates a tender or load. The API validates tenant, organisation, lane, volume, service window and commercial policy before accepting the command.

3

Marketplace services mediate competition. Eligible transporters see opportunities, submit rates or bids, and receive an award or allocation produced by platform policy.

4

Edge inputs are normalized. Driver apps, fleet systems, GPS providers and optional leaf nodes are adapters; their raw payloads never become the public domain contract.

5

Trip services derive shared operational truth. Milestones, latest position, ETA and exceptions are projected into the permitted shipper, transporter and operator views.

6

Bulk evidence bypasses NATS payload storage. The evidence service records version, checksum and policy metadata in Postgres, then uses a provider-neutral adapter and presigned URLs for regional S3-compatible document storage.

7

Acceptance gates settlement. Claims and proof approval determine whether rating and invoicing may progress; no client may publish its own settlement outcome.

8

The marketplace closes the loop. Payment status, remittance, audit and analytics return to role-filtered client experiences without direct customer-to-transporter data access.

Multi-region Tenant Authority and Deployment

This architecture separates the logical trust hierarchy and shared control plane from two repeatable physical regional cells. A tenant account can be resolved in permitted regions without making region part of tenant identity.

1

The NATS operator is the global trust root. Regional servers resolve the same signed account and user claims; trust is not recreated independently per region.

2

Each marketplace tenant receives one NATS account. Its subject space and users are isolated from every other tenant unless a named import and export deliberately connects them.

3

Membership becomes a scoped credential. Tenant, organisation, role and business context determine short-lived user and workload grants with automatic renewal.

4

The control plane is shared. It manages tenants and organisations, identity, NATS provisioning, reference configuration, entitlements, billing, administration, observability and audit.

5

A regional cell is repeatable. Regional ingress, service replicas, three NATS nodes, JetStream, service-owned databases and an S3-compatible evidence-store binding deploy as one versioned unit.

6

Region A normally owns local work. Queue workers, streams and databases stay near the tenant's operational data while account authority remains portable.

7

Region B repeats the same contracts. It can host locally placed tenants, active capacity or a policy-defined recovery posture without becoming a bespoke platform.

8

Gateway reachability is live and interest-based. A supercluster joins independent regional clusters; it does not turn the WAN into one stretched cluster.

9

Durable movement is explicit. Selected JetStream mirrors, database backups and S3 object replication follow residency and recovery policy, separately from gateway routing.

10

Recovery is a governed promotion. Tenant-specific RTO, RPO and permitted regions determine routing, data promotion and re-entry into the home cell.

Logical authority and physical placement are deliberately side by side; neither axis is encoded inside the other.Connected Freight Reference Architecture

Connected Fleet Data Gathering, Processing and Use

This architecture follows vehicle data from truck sensors, driver applications and fleet providers through secure onboarding, regional NATS ingestion, normalization, state projection and role-filtered consumer services.

1

The truck produces several evidence types. Position, ignition and component health originate in vehicle systems; the driver adds task completion, exception and POD evidence.

2

The edge buffers and authenticates. A vehicle gateway or provider agent handles intermittent cellular service, ordering and secure delivery without becoming the business system of record.

3

Onboarding binds technical identity to business identity. Vehicle, provider, tenant, transporter organisation, consent and retention policy are established before telemetry is admitted.

4

Routing selects a permitted regional endpoint. The tenant's placement policy determines home and recovery cells; the NATS account remains the authority wherever it connects.

5

Adapters authenticate, validate and deduplicate. Every observation keeps provider provenance, source timestamp, accuracy and sequence information for later diagnosis.

6

NATS separates live delivery from durable history. Core request/reply and notifications serve live work; JetStream retains replayable position and exception facts in the regional domain.

7

Transactional state remains service-owned. Postgres owns current decisions, while KV and projections provide fast current-state reads without replacing the authority.

8

Bulk evidence uses a storage adapter. POD, scans and diagnostic files go to regional S3-compatible storage; Postgres retains metadata. NATS Object Store remains optional for small operational artifacts, not the bulk archive.

9

Operational views are role-filtered. Shippers see allocated-load progress, transporters see their fleet and jobs, and operators see cross-platform health through approved operational exports.

10

External consumers receive contracts, not internals. Customer systems use APIs, webhooks or approved event products with explicit versions, scopes and delivery guarantees.

Security, Privacy and Regulatory Controls

This control view places identity, authorization, tenant separation, encryption, privacy, residency and audit around the same NATS-based logistics platform. It defines architectural requirements and evidence points; it does not claim certification or replace jurisdiction-specific legal and security review.

1

Federation proves identity; membership grants tenancy. WorkOS or an enterprise IdP authenticates people, while platform records bind each user to permitted tenant organisations.

2

RBAC supplies the stable baseline. Shipper, transporter, driver, finance, support and platform roles grant understandable job-level permissions inside an organisation.

3

ABAC narrows each decision. Tenant, organisation, resource ownership, tender or trip allocation, region, data class and risk context constrain what the role may do now.

4

The NATS operator anchors messaging trust. It signs one account per marketplace tenant; organisation membership and roles stay inside that tenant's application authority.

5

Tenant accounts deny cross-tenant access by default. Subjects, streams, limits and credentials are isolated. Any marketplace sharing uses an explicit, reviewed import/export contract.

6

Connections and stored data are encrypted. HTTPS, WSS, TLS and mTLS protect transit; databases, JetStream volumes, backups and S3-compatible evidence stores use managed keys with rotation.

7

Sensitive fields are removed before logs leave the service. Secrets, tokens, personal data, proof-of-delivery content and precise locations are masked, tokenized or omitted by policy.

8

Residency is a placement rule, not a label. Tenant contract, jurisdiction, data class, retention and approved cross-border transfer determine the region for streams, databases, S3 buckets and backups.

9

Audit remains tenant-aware and tamper-resistant. Authentication, authorization decisions, administrative actions, credential changes and sensitive reads produce correlated evidence without exposing payload data.

10

Response can contain one tenant or credential. Operators can suspend an account, revoke or rotate credentials, isolate exports, preserve evidence and replay approved events without opening every tenant.

Control intent must be traced to threat models, data classification, tenant contracts, retention schedules and the regulations applicable in each operating region.Connected Freight Reference Architecture

Regional Deployment and Country-Resident Tenant Placement

The baseline uses three-node NATS clusters in Cape Town and Sydney, joined as a supercluster. A Botswana-regulated tenant is placed in a separate in-country sovereign cell: application transit, JetStream, databases, evidence, logs, keys and backups stay inside Botswana, with cross-border data paths denied by default.

1

Authority is logical; regulated data is local. The shared control plane does not store, relay or administer Botswana freight, identity, evidence, log or credential data; those functions execute inside Botswana.

2

Placement is policy, not a cross-border runtime link. The Botswana-local registry is authoritative for home and recovery placement. A global directory may retain only a non-sensitive deployment alias if regulation permits.

3

Southern Africa runs in Cape Town. AWS af-south-1 supplies three Availability Zones for one NATS server per zone, local services and local data.

4

Australia initially runs in Sydney. AWS ap-southeast-2 supplies the equivalent three-zone cell; Melbourne can be evaluated later without changing application contracts.

5

JetStream replication remains local. Each cluster uses R=3 inside its allowed placement boundary; consensus never spans the intercontinental or Botswana border links.

6

Cape Town and Sydney form the shared supercluster. Gateways carry remote interest between eligible accounts. Botswana accounts and subjects are deliberately absent from that gateway path.

7

Each cell owns its durable state. Postgres holds transactional truth, JetStream holds replayable facts, and the storage adapter writes bulk evidence to locally approved S3-compatible storage.

8

Botswana is a sovereign deployment cell. Local tenant administration, identity, credentials, ingress, services, NATS, JetStream, databases, evidence, masked logs, keys, backups and recovery all remain in-country.

9

Country and tenant remain separate axes. Regulation can force Botswana placement and a silo without redefining every Botswana operating organisation as a marketplace tenant.

10

Cross-border movement is deny-by-default. No gateway, mirror, database replica, document copy, log export or foreign failover exists for Botswana. Any masked aggregate egress is an explicit policy exception.
