What request/reply covers

A client publishes to a subject and waits on a private, single-use reply subject it generates itself — _INBOX.<nuid>. Whoever answers publishes the reply to that inbox, and only the original client is subscribed to it, so the response routes back to exactly the requester who asked. No application-level correlation bookkeeping is needed for the network hop itself.

In this lab that pattern carries two subject families: rpc.* (service-to-service calls) and api.* (browser-to-service calls). Reply-routing and request-tracing solve different problems, and NATS only automates the first one — so every hop also carries a W3C traceparent header: a traceId shared by the whole call, plus a spanId the receiving hop mints for itself and a parentSpanId copied from the caller's own spanId. That parent/child pair is what lets the Traces tab rebuild the call tree — including any further hop a service makes on its own behalf — even though NATS itself never looks inside the header. correlationId is carried too, but it is a separate per-hop field, not the span id.
