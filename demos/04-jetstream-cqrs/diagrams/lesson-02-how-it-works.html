One consumer, many workers

The other tabs each answer one question with one number. This page is the mechanism behind all four: where the speed comes from, what it costs, and which dial moves the cost. Nothing here is measured — press Run on any tab for numbers, and they will be your own.

The fold loses an event when two events of the SAME vehicle are in flight at the same time, and the later one finishes first. Everything below is an illustration of that one sentence.

One stream, many workers

Workers do not each get a copy of the log. They share one durable consumer, and the server hands each waiting worker the next event. That is the whole of the speed-up: folding happens on several workers at once instead of one after another.

Where the order is lost

A fold per vehicle only accepts an event that is ahead of what it has already folded. Two events of one vehicle in flight together is the whole risk — if the later one lands first, the earlier one arrives behind the watermark and is refused.

Redelivery

A worker that takes an event and then goes quiet has not lost it. The server waits out AckWait and hands the same event to another worker. On this lesson that rescue almost always arrives too late to be worth anything.

What MaxAckPending caps

It caps the number of events allowed to be unacked at once, for the whole consumer. It does not hand work out per worker, and it does not leave a worker idle for ever. Narrow it and the order gets safer while the throughput falls.

So what do you do with it

  • If the order matters, do not race it. One worker per key, or one worker, or a design where two events of one key cannot be in flight together. The pool here is wrong on purpose so the cost is visible.
  • If the throughput matters and the order does not — independent events, no per-key fold — the pool is exactly right, and the tabs measure how much it buys.
  • Do not read a probability off this page. It is a race between two folds, and the winner depends on timing this demo cannot promise. The dials tell you which way the risk moves. The Run buttons tell you what happened on your machine.
