One Namespace,

Each

Fourteen cards from nine runnable topologies. Cards 01 to 06 build the words, simple first: the map, Raft, placement, accounts and domains. Cards 07 to 12 use those words on each shape. Card 13 tests one procedure for moving between two of them. Card 14 asks where T4’s meta leader should live, and whether it stays there.

The question every card serves

Two regions, one platform. Do you want one global stream name, or do you want each region to keep working when the other goes dark? With two sites, no shape in this lab gives both. A third site that votes keeps one meta group alive when either region is dark (card 08). A stream stored in the dark region is still dark with it.

Four sentences the deck depends on

A gateway joins clusters into one supercluster with one meta group. A leaf link, as this lab builds it — $SYS not carried, a domain per side — leaves a meta group on each side. An account is the wall for data. A domain names a JetStream, and on a $SYS leaf it can stop JetStream extending across the link; it is still not a security boundary.

Quorum — Raft

A vote needs more than half, and servers vote, not sites

JetStream keeps its state in Raft groups. A Raft group is a set of servers that elect one leader. The leader can act only while more than half of the group can hear it. That number is the quorum, or majority. There is more than one kind of group, and this card counts the meta group.

Decision

Before you pick a shape, count the servers in the meta group. Ask: when one site goes dark, is more than half of it still alive? If not, it stops. Sites do not vote; servers do.

Three kinds of Raft group — each elects its own leader and counts its own majority
GroupHow manyIts serversWhat stops when it loses its majority
Meta group — this cardone per JetStreamevery server in that JetStreamcreate, update and delete of streams and consumers (A11)
Stream groupone per streamits 1, 3 or 5 replicas, in one cluster (F17)storing and reading that one stream (A21)
Consumer groupone per consumerthe servers of its stream (A18)delivery and acks for that one consumer documented
  • The meta leader is not a stream leader. Each group holds its own vote documented. With no meta leader, a publish into an existing stream is stored only while that stream's own group keeps its majority (A12, F21). Card 03 asks all three questions.
Meta group majority, then a dark site — green is live, red is dark, grey is the rest

An even split of an even group stops both halves. Three sites break the tie; two sites cannot.

Mechanism
  • Raft keeps one ordered log per group. The leader adds an entry; the entry is committed when a majority has stored it. The docs give quorum as half the cluster size, plus one documented.
  • A majority is always more than half. So two groups of servers that cannot reach each other can never both hold a majority of the same group. One side, at most, keeps a leader.
  • An uneven split can leave one side a majority: 6 of 9 keeps a leader (F3). An even split of an even group leaves no side one: 3 of 6 has no leader (A10).
  • Every dark site in this lab is kill -STOP. A live WAN cut, where both sides run and cannot talk, uses the same arithmetic, but it was not built inferred.

What it does give you
  • The arithmetic is exact. Count the live servers in a group and you know if it can act. Every leader and no-leader result above was measured.
  • At most one side keeps a leader. Raft does not let two leaders change the same group.

  • A named leader is not readiness. /jsz can name a new leader before management calls succeed; a create asked at once was refused (F6a). Call it ready only when a real operation, such as a stream create, succeeds. Retry, and record the wait.
Verdict

Put the majority where the work must go on. Card 03 shows that there is more than one group to count.

Quorum — three kinds of Raft group

"Can we still write?" is three questions

Card 02 counted the meta group. The other two kinds, one group per stream and one per consumer, count their own majority. So in a gateway shape with a region dark, one question fails and the others can still work.

Decision

Before you accept any topology, say which of the three you need during an outage. Losing the meta group is a change freeze, not a write freeze. They are not the same outage.

One rig, three answers — T2 gateway, ZA frozen, AU live

The same drawing, three different answers. The shape is not "up" or "down".

Placement — where a stream lands

Placement decides where bytes sit, not who survives

Every stream is placed once, when it is created, into one cluster. Ask for nothing and it lands where the client is. Name a cluster and it lands there. T4 is the reference here: three clusters, one meta group of 9.

Decision

For every stream, KV bucket and consumer, say which cluster it belongs in, and make the create call say so. A stream stored in the dark region is dark with it, whatever the quorum does.

T4 — what landed where, with no region dark

The arbiter votes and stores nothing — until somebody places a stream on it.

Mechanism
  • Placement belongs to a stream, not to an account. Two streams in one account can sit in two clusters: --cluster za and --cluster au from the same client both work (E13, E14).
  • This lab set the initial placement, at create time. A stream config can name a cluster or tags documented. This lab tested --cluster only; tags were not tested. NATS also documents changing placement later, which moves the replicas documented (docs: placement). Moving a stream was not tested here.
  • With no placement, the stream lands in the client's own cluster. The meta leader records the choice; where the meta leader sits does not pick the cluster inferred — no check moved the leader to test it.
  • Placement is not quorum. T4 survives a dark region because of the meta majority (card 02). A stream placed in that dark region is still dark.
  • Control path and data path differ. A read from AU of a stream in za gives 1 / 1 (F13, A26): that is one stream, read over the WAN (C13a), not two copies. A second copy in au needs a mirror (A15, card 10).

What it does NOT give you

  • A consumer on a far stream pays the WAN on every pull (A19).
  • The arbiter stores whatever is placed on it (C8, C10). A 3-node arbiter could even take an R3 stream inferred.
Verdict

Name the cluster in every create call, and keep clients off the arbiter's port (C11).

Boundaries — accounts and the JetStream namespace

A stream name is unique in one meta group and one account

Two things decide whether two regions can both own a stream called ODOMETER: how many meta groups there are, and whether they share an account. One meta group × one account is one JetStream namespace. Only there does a name clash.

Decision

Give each region its own account unless you truly want one name for both regions. Then pick the link. The account choice works the same on every shape; the link choice does not.

Two choices, four outcomes — one publish in za, then count

Same number, opposite meaning. 1 / 1 over a gateway is one copy. 1 / 1 over this lab's leaf is two.

Mechanism
  • An account owns its subjects, streams and consumers. Nothing crosses to another account without an export and an import (card 11).
  • A meta group keeps one catalogue. Inside it, one account can hold one stream per name. So the unit where a name must be unique is one meta group × one account: the JetStream namespace.
  • A domain name does not split it. Variant B put two domain names on one meta group and still got 10058 (B8). Card 06.
  • The meta group is not per account. T2 has one meta group with three JetStream accounts on (A1). So a dark region freezes creates in every account at the same moment inferred. An account is a wall for data, not for availability.
  • On T5 the leaf binds LB, so a subject crosses the link, and each meta group's ODOMETER stores the publish (D5). Neither can refuse the other's name: two same-named shadow streams hold 1 / 2 (D6).
Read a count by its shape, not by its number

What it does give you
  • An account per region behaves the same on every shape. Own names, own data, 1 / 0 (A6 A7, D18, H6).
  • A shared account over a gateway gives one global name and stores one copy (A4, G7a).
What it does NOT give you
  • A shared account over this lab's leaf captures twice, silently (D5, D6).
  • Over a gateway, AU's stream info ODOMETER returns za's stream (A5). It looks local. It is not.
  • Per-region accounts do not share data. Sharing needs a mirror or an export (cards 10, 11).
Verdict

The account is the wall. The meta group decides how big the room is.

Boundaries — domains and leaf links

A domain names a JetStream. It is not a wall.

jetstream.domain gives one JetStream a name and an API prefix, $JS.<domain>.API. It does not decide which servers vote together: the links and the system account ($SYS) decide that. A domain string alone does not make a meta group.

Decision

Inside one supercluster, use one domain name everywhere, or none. Across a leaf link, a domain per side is how you name, and reach, each JetStream. Never use a domain as a security boundary.

Three rigs — what the domain did, and what the link did

An account (the yellow box on card 05) stops data. A domain (the orange box here) names a JetStream. It is not a wall. Neither box decides who votes.

Mechanism
  • Routes and gateways join servers into one JetStream with one meta group. A leaf link joins the JetStream on each side only when it carries the system account and neither side sets a different domain documented (docs: leaf nodes).
  • This lab never carries $SYS over a leaf. With a domain per side, that gave three JetStreams (D1, H1). With no domain at all, the hub still stayed separate (G1, G2). So in this lab the missing $SYS link is enough on its own inferred.
  • A stream name resolves in the local domain (H7a). To reach another JetStream, name its API: external.api = $JS.za.API (D8, H11). external.api is a JetStream API prefix. In this lab the prefix names a domain, and binding an account per region changed nothing (H11a). The prefix can also name an API imported from another account documented; that was not configured here.
  • Leave out external.api and a mirror either copies nothing (D7) or silently copies a local stream of the same name (D9a). No error either way (D7a).

What it does give you — use a domain when
  • You need to reach another JetStream by name: $JS.<domain>.API, or external.api on a mirror (D8, H11).
  • You want a leaf side to keep its own JetStream even if $SYS is carried documented.
What it does NOT give you
  • Not a security boundary. Anyone with the account can name the other API and read it, as D8 and H11 do on purpose.
  • Different names inside one supercluster break it at start-up, and which side breaks is a coin toss (B1, G19a).
Verdict

Accounts decide who may see what. Links and $SYS decide who votes with whom. A domain names the result, and on a $SYS leaf it decides whether JetStream extends or stays separate. It is never a wall.

Links — T2 / A, the gateway

A gateway buys one name and sells your survival

A gateway joins two clusters into one supercluster. With JetStream on every server and no domain, the six servers form one JetStream with one meta group. One set of stream names per account, and one majority, held across the sea.

Decision

Use a gateway when you want one stream name per account, everywhere, and you can add a third site that votes (card 08). Do not use a plain two-region gateway if either region must keep creating streams while the other is dark.

The shape, and what it does when ZA goes dark

One frame, six servers, one vote. Half the vote is in the other region.

Mechanism
  • A gateway joins clusters into one supercluster. With JetStream on all six servers and no domain, they elect one meta group of six (A1, A2). One domain name on all six gives the same one JetStream, with a name documented. Different names break it (card 06).
  • Creating a stream needs 4 of 6. Each region holds 3. With ZA dark there is no meta leader (A10) and every create fails (A11). The lab froze ZA only. AU dark is the same arithmetic inferred.
  • The meta group is not per account (A1: one group, three JetStream accounts). So the freeze hits every account at once inferred.
  • A stream's replicas stay in one cluster (A22–A24). AU's streams keep their own majority, so a publish into them is stored (A12). Card 03.
  • After ZA comes back, the group is 6 again (A13) and the one stream checked still holds its message (A14). Consumers and KV were not checked after recovery.
What it does give you
  • One name means one stream, in one account, everywhere. No second ODOMETER by accident (A4).
  • A mirror in the other region copies with no external.api (A15, A16, A17a). There is one JetStream to look in.
  • Any client can place a stream in either region with --cluster (E13, E14). Card 04.
What it does NOT give you
  • Two regions of three cannot out-vote each other. Lose one and nobody can create, update or delete a stream.
  • No second ODOMETER in a shared account. AU's is refused with 10058 (A4). Fix: give each region its own account. Then each region creates its own (A6, A7). Card 05.
  • A plain nats pub is a core publish and exits 0 either way. Count the stream to know (A12).
  • Streams stored in the dark region cannot be read (A21). AU's stream info on a za stream looks local; it is not (A5).
What the drawing does not show
  • This is a frozen region, not a split. In a real WAN cut both sides stay alive. A 3 / 3 split leaves neither side a majority; an uneven split can leave one side a majority. Two sides can never both hold a majority of one group inferred. That rig is not built here.
  • The leader field in /jsz goes stale for a while after a majority dies (A10a). Wait for it; never read it once.
  • A new leader is named before it will take a change. Ask at once and even a healthy group refuses (F6a). Wait for that too.
Verdict

A two-region gateway is one JetStream wearing two coats. Add a third site that votes (card 08), or accept a change freeze when either region is dark.

Quorum — T3 / C and T4 / F, the arbiter

An arbiter site buys the vote back

Two regions of three cannot out-vote each other. Three sites can. Add a third cluster on the same gateway that votes and stores almost nothing. The meta group grows to 9, and either region alone can go dark.

Decision

If you want one stream name across regions and creates during a region outage, pay for a third site. A three-node arbiter is the safe size. Keep clients off it.

T4 — nine servers, three sites, one meta group — and AU goes dark

The middle box is the purchase. It stores nothing and it is why the left box can still create.

Mechanism
  • The meta group counts servers, not sites (card 02). Nine need five. Either region dark leaves six (F3, F22).
  • The arbiter only has to be reachable over the gateway and vote. It holds no copy, KV or consumer unless one is placed on it (F20a, C20a). Card 04.
  • A stream keeps its own group. Even with no meta leader, a publish into an existing stream is stored while that stream's own group keeps its majority (F9, F21). Card 03.
  • The arbiter does not copy data across regions. Every replica of a stream stays in one cluster (F17). So a stream stored in the dark region is dark too, even while the vote lives (F28). The region's own streams still read (F27). Card 03.

Links — T5 / D and H, hub and leaf

This lab's leaf link gives each region a vote, and a double copy

Each region hangs off a 3-node hub on a leafnode link. As this lab builds it, $SYS is not carried over the leaf and each side sets its own domain. The result is three JetStreams, each with its own meta group of 3.

Decision

Use this shape when each region must keep creating streams alone. Accept that one stream name no longer means one stream, and write every shared copy down on purpose.

T5 — three JetStreams, and what they do when AU goes dark

Three orange boxes, three votes. And one account box in each, so one send is stored twice.

Mechanism
  • Two settings made three JetStreams here: $SYS is not carried over the leaf, and each side sets a domain (D1). With no domain at all the hub still stayed separate (G1, G2), so the $SYS setting did the separating inferred. Card 06.
  • This is not the only way a leaf can work. A leaf that carries $SYS and sets no domain extends a clustered hub's JetStream instead documented. Not tested here.
  • Each region's meta group is 3 servers at home, quorum 2 (D2). Another site going dark changes nothing (D24–D26, D10–D13).
  • A leaf remote binds one account (D22a). The lab bound LB, so its subjects cross the link, and each JetStream stores its own copy (D5). Nobody can refuse the name (D3, D6).
  • A stream in another domain is reached through its API: external.api = $JS.au.API (D8). Without it a mirror copies nothing, or silently copies a local stream of the same name (D7a, D9a).
Variant H — the same shape, an account per region
  • A second leaf remote per region server binds LB_ZA or LB_AU to the hub. Three JetStreams still (H1). One publish in LB_ZA reads 1 / 0 (H6). The hub holds three namespaces, not one shared one (H9a). A hub stream on the same subject still captures the live publish (H8): H stops ZA / AU cross-capture, not capture at the hub.
  • With the whole hub gone, a region still creates and stores (H12–H14, H16). A region dark was not run for H. What H buys is a live subject feed to the hub, and nothing more (H15).
What it does give you
  • Each region creates and stores alone (D25, D26).
  • The hub is a reader, not a dependency. Kill it and both regions carry on (D10–D13, H14a).
  • No shared majority, so a region can be added without a re-vote inferred.
What it does NOT give you
  • A shared account over this leaf captures twice, silently (D5, D6).
  • That double capture is live only, not replication. A message sent while the link is down reaches one side, and nothing catches up later. Subject capture is not outage recovery. Only a mirror copies history inferred.
  • Nothing is shared until somebody writes it down (D15). A mirror is a real thing to build and watch (card 10).
  • A name-only mirror can copy the wrong stream with no error (D9a).
Verdict

For two regions that must each keep working, start here — with an account per region, and every shared stream a written mirror.

Copying — mirrors

A mirror is the second copy — and it can copy the wrong stream

A mirror names a source stream. The name is looked up in the asking side's own JetStream and own account. If the source is anywhere else, the mirror must say where. If it does not, it is still accepted — and copies the wrong thing, or nothing.

Decision

Every mirror whose source is in another JetStream sets mirror.external.api. Treat a mirror that has never been tested by counting as a bug, even when it is green.

The same config, one line apart

Both drawings are a working mirror. Only the top one is the mirror you meant.

Mechanism
  • A mirror is a stream that copies another stream, one way. The copy is read-only.
  • A bare source name is looked up in the asking side: its own JetStream and its own account (H7a, E18a). Card 05 is the same fact seen from the other end.
  • mirror.external.api tells the mirror which JetStream API to ask. In this lab it names a domain prefix (H11a). No gateway, no shared vote.
  • A name-only mirror cannot cross an account (E17, E18). NATS documents a cross-account mirror: the owner exports its JetStream API, the other account imports it, and the mirror names it in external.api documented (docs: cross-account). Not configured or measured here. The measured way across an account is to export the subject (card 11).
  • The lab proves each copy by counting messages on both sides, never by the mirror's status — the wrong mirror reports healthy too (D7a, E18a).

Copying — T2 / E, export and import

Export / import: the explicit way across an account

An account shares nothing by accident. To let one account see another's messages, the owner exports and the guest imports — two edits, in two places, by two people.

Decision

Share between tenants with export / import and an import prefix. Never by putting two tenants in one account, and never by hoping a domain will keep them apart (card 06).

One publish, and where it lands

Two yellow boxes, one thin arrow, and everything that is not that arrow does not cross.

Mechanism
  • The owner declares an export naming exactly the subjects it is willing to share. Nothing else is offered.
  • The guest declares an import and gives it a prefix. The message arrives on from_za.odo.>, not on odo.>.
  • The prefix keeps the two apart: imported traffic arrives on subjects of its own (E3, E3a). It is not proof of origin. A local user can still publish onto from_za.> unless its permissions deny that prefix inferred. The rig set no such permission.
  • Sharing a subject is not sharing a stream. The guest still cannot ask about the owner's stream at all (E6), and its own same-named stream is unrelated (E7, E8).
  • This was measured over a gateway, in one JetStream. The export rule is an account rule, so it does not depend on the link documented.
What it does give you
  • Nothing crosses unless two people agree. The owner offers, the guest accepts.
  • The share is narrow. One subject pattern, not an account.
  • The prefix gives imported traffic a namespace of its own. It does not prove origin; reserve the prefix with publish permissions.
What it does NOT give you
  • It is two edits in two configs. Get either half wrong and it shares nothing inferred.
  • It shares live subjects, not history. A new guest sees nothing sent before it imported documented.
  • Every consumer on the guest side must use the prefixed subject.
Why this card sits next to card 10
  • Card 10 shows a name-only mirror failing silently across an account wall (E17, E18). This card is the measured way to move data across an account. NATS also documents a mirror across accounts, through an exported JetStream API documented (docs: cross-account). This lab did not try it.
Verdict

Every account crossing is an export / import. This lab measured it on subjects. It is deliberately small, and that is the point.

Traps — T6 / G, both links at once

Both links at once is not both shapes at once

Add a hub leaf link to a gateway pair and it looks like belt and braces. It is not. The gateway decides (G24). You pay for a hub, and the two regions get no vote back.

Decision

Pick one link type between the two regions. If they share a gateway, a leaf-linked hub cannot rescue them, however healthy it is.

A healthy hub, and a region that still cannot create

The teal lines are real, and the blue line still decides. A hub on a leaf has no vote in a gateway's meta group.

Mechanism
  • The gateway still fuses za and au into one meta group of 6 (G2, G3). The leaf links do not undo that.
  • The hub stays its own JetStream of 3 with no domain set (G1). The leaf does not carry $SYS, and a leaf joins a JetStream only when it does documented. Card 06.
  • With AU dark, ZA holds 3 of 6 and cannot reach 4 (G10, G11). The hub has a leader (G13), but it is not in that group, so it cannot lend a vote (G14a).
  • Phase 2 adds a domain per site. It breaks the way variant B breaks: one region blind, still one group of 6, 10005 on the blind side (G19–G22). The leaf does not rescue it. Only the hub, across the leaf, keeps its own leader (G23).
Moving between shapes — what was measured, and what was not
  • A leaf beside the gateway is coexistence, not a conversion. It changes nothing (G24). A domain per site on the gateway breaks it (G19–G22).
  • Switching T4 ↔ T5 in place is card 13. The one procedure tested did not establish safe conversion. Other procedures are untested, so the choice is not one-way.
  • If the goal is a third site that votes, join it with a gateway, not a leaf. That is card 08, and it is measured.
What it does give you — the short list
  • A hub gives one more place to attach clients that is not either region.
  • The hub keeps a working JetStream of its own, and can die with no effect on either region (G16–G18).
What it does NOT give you
  • Every T5 property is gone. 10058 is back (G4), one copy not two (G7a), and a dark region freezes creates (G11).
  • You pay for a third site that cannot lend a vote (G14a).
  • On a diagram it looks safer than a plain gateway. That is the trap.
Verdict

A leaf link beside a gateway buys the two regions nothing. Do not build it.

Moving — T4 / S and T5 / S, switch in place · D03-R11 · D03-R12

The tested stop–rewrite–restart procedure did not establish safe conversion

One procedure was tested, both ways between T4 and T5: stop all nine servers, rewrite the configs, start them again on the same stores. It ran in three cases, on 2026-09-28 and again on 2026-09-29. It failed in every case, both days. This card is about that one procedure, not about every way to move.

Decision

Choose T4 or T5 before the data lands. If you must move later, do not rely on the tested procedure. Another way may work, but nobody has tested one.

Three cases, two runs, one procedure

The same procedure gave different results on two days. A procedure you cannot repeat is not a procedure you can run on stores you need.

Mechanism — what each case did
  • A · T4 → T5. The servers kept the old membership. Every site still reported nine members, and no site elected a meta leader for minutes, after the switch and after a restart (SA7, SA10, SA28, SA29). On 2026-09-29 each stream group still took acked publishes, and every message, reader position and KV key read back correct (SA11–SA27). On 2026-09-28 the read needed a meta leader, so the data was not read: unverified, not disproved.
  • B1 · T5 → T4, clean names. One group of nine formed both times (SB10, SB29). On 2026-09-28 the ZA streams could not be read and took no new writes. On 2026-09-29 every checked value matched. The one unmet check is a placement read that came back empty right after the switch; it read za after the restart (SB18, SB37).
  • B2 · T5 → T4, one name in both regions. In T5 each region may own a stream called ODOMETER in account LB. In T4 the account has one namespace, so the two names collide. The ZA lookup resolved to AU's stream (SC36, SC51). za-2, then za-1, removed their copies of every ZA stream; za-3 kept its copy. With one copy of three, those streams took no writes (SC29, SC33, SC35). After the restart no replica answered for two of them: inconclusive (SC42, SC48).
  • In both T5 → T4 cases, AU kept working on both days. In case A on 2026-09-28, AU's data was not read either.
What this card does not say
  • It does not make T4 or T5 a one-way choice. One procedure failed. Other procedures are untested. proposed Build the new shape beside the old one, copy each stream across, move clients region by region, remove the old link last. Nobody has run this.
  • It does not say why the ZA replicas were removed. The cause is not proved.
  • Three different things, and only two were seen. Replica removal was seen: za-2 and za-1 removed their copies. Unavailable data was seen: those streams took no writes, and after the restart no replica answered for two of them (SC42, SC48). Irrecoverable loss is neither proved nor disproved.
  • A surviving copy proves nothing by itself. Files remain on za-3. That does not show the data in them is whole, and it does not show they can be brought back. Neither was tested.
  • One failure is the test's own. The probe acked through the ZA name, so AU's reader moved on by one (SC38, SC56). That is a side effect of the lab, not of NATS.
  • Provenance. The 2026-09-29 verdicts were judged live. The 2026-09-28 verdicts were derived from its evidence afterwards. The report's check IDs are the second run's.
What held
  • The rig checks. All passed in every case: no checked setup or harness requirement failed. That does not make every failed check the conversion's. One came from the test probe (SC38, SC56).
  • AU, and every stream stored there, in both T5 → T4 cases, both days.
  • On 2026-09-29, the data in A and B1: messages, reader positions and KV keys (SA11–SA39, SB11–SB39).
What broke
  • T4 → T5 never reached T5, on either day (SA10, SA29).
  • T5 → T4 is not repeatable. In B1 the ZA streams were gone one day, and matched the next.
  • A name in both regions collides in T4 (SC36, SC51). ZA's replicas were removed, and those streams became unavailable (SC29, SC42).
Verdict

Do not rely on the tested stop–rewrite–restart procedure to switch T4 and T5. Pick the shape first. Before any attempt to merge into T4, resolve same-name streams in each shared account — but unique names did not make it safe: B1 had clean names, and on 2026-09-28 its ZA streams were gone.

Quorum — T4 / F, the meta leader in the hub · D03-R13 – D03-R18

Hub placement is a request; regional recovery can change leadership

Hub leadership can be requested, but regional recovery can disturb it. Metadata quorum and stream availability remain separate. Measured on 2026-10-05 with lab/10-hub-meta-leader.sh; every count is in REPORT-10.md, tied to a named run.

Decision

If you want the meta leader in the hub, ask for it with a step-down, and ask again after every regional recovery. It is a request, not a setting. Plan a metadata outage and a stream outage as two different things.

Measured, run 10-20261005-150304 — meta group and streams, shown apart

A vote does not carry data, and data does not carry a vote. The hub can keep writing with no meta leader, and a region can lose writes with a healthy meta group.

Mechanism — what the machine did
  • Start-up does not put the leader in the hub. Nothing in the config pins it (ML5a). A step-down with --cluster arb moved it there every time it was asked (ML26, ML36, ML93, ML111).
  • One region dark leaves six voters, above the quorum of five. A hub leader held, in the same term, and creates went on (ML44, ML49a, ML61, ML66a). A leader in the dark region was replaced from outside it (ML78, ML96).
  • Both regions dark leaves three voters. The hub is healthy, but it has no meta leader and a create fails (ML116, ML117, ML120). Its own stream still takes writes and reads back (ML118, ML119).
  • On a return, recovery and stability are two answers. Every valid round recovered: peers current, one leader, creates work. Stability did not always hold. The leader left the hub — to the region that stayed up (ML54, ML71) or to the returning one (ML106), or the same server came back in a higher term (ML157a).
What this card does not say
  • It does not say why a return disturbs leadership. The cause is not proved.
  • It gives no rate. The rounds are few. The cross-run table in REPORT-10.md counts them per named run; quote that table, never a percentage.
  • A timed-out publish is an unknown outcome, not a failure. The CLI gave up (ML140a, ML151a). In the cited run nothing was stored later (ML146a, ML157a), but in the earlier step-6 own-rig run one such message was. Why is not proved. Retry with the same Nats-Msg-Id.
  • A leader field can be stale. After both regions went dark, the hub servers stopped naming the old leader one at a time (ML116a). Read every hub monitor, and wait.
  • Provenance. The figure is run 10-20261005-150304. Runs with a failed rig precondition count as inconclusive, never as a result.
What held
  • The request. A hub step-down put the leader in the hub, every trial (ML26, ML111).
  • One region dark, leader in the hub: the leader stayed, metadata and writes went on (ML44, ML61).
  • Recovery, in every valid round (ML53, ML131).
What broke
  • Stability on return. The leader moved, or the term rose (ML54, ML71, ML106, ML157a).
  • The hub alone cannot change metadata. Both regions dark: no leader, no create (ML116, ML120).
  • A dark region's stream takes no write, even with a healthy meta group (ML140, ML151).
Verdict

Request hub leadership; do not depend on it. Re-request it after each regional recovery, and alert on where the meta leader is. Size each stream's replicas for the outage it must survive: the meta quorum will not do that for you.
