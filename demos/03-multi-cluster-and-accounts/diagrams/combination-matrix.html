  • Only one thing decides whether ODOMETER can exist twice: is there a second namespace? A gateway does not make one — P3, P5, P7 are all refused with 10058. A second account makes one (P4, P6, P8). No link at all makes one (P1, P2). A leaf link makes one, because a domain may change across it (P9, P10).
  • The arbiter is orthogonal. It changes quorum only. Compare P3 with P5 and P7: the stream answer never moves. Compare P5 with P7: 1 instance survives a region loss with no slack, 3 instances survive with one node to spare. That is the whole difference.
  • An arbiter site is still a JetStream peer. Keep data off it with placement (--cluster za|au), or it will host streams you did not plan for.
  • Replicas never cross a region. All of a stream's replicas live inside one cluster, in every row above. An arbiter does not give you a third copy of your data.
  • P4 is the recommendation for two regions: one shared domain lb, a gateway, and one account per region. P8 is P4 plus quorum insurance. P9 is the only row that double-captures, and it does so because you gave up the shared subject space.

The second axis — replicas, inside one cluster

Replicas are not a region setting. Every replica of a stream lives inside one cluster. Replicas: 3 means three copies in za, not one in each region. That is why row 1's AU read stays a WAN query no matter what you set here.

What the matrix says in one paragraph

  • A domain cannot split a supercluster. Row 5. A domain name may only change across a leaf-node link, and a gateway is not one. Use the same domain everywhere — lb.
  • Inside one account, over a gateway, a publish can never be stored twice. Rows 1, 2, 3 and 5. Same name is refused, overlapping subjects are refused, disjoint subjects mean only one filter matches.
  • A second copy needs a second namespace. Rows 7, 8 and 9 — a second JetStream system, a mirror, or a second account with an explicit crossing.
  • The account is what lets a region own its own data. Row 4 is the recommendation. Placement (--cluster za|au) then decides which cluster holds it.
  • Row 6 and row 7 are not rivals. The arbiter keeps one shared subject space and rescues the quorum. Hub-and-leaf gives up the shared space and removes the shared quorum. Pick by what you would rather own: less config, or less coupling.
Still open. Row 9 is the only unmeasured claim in the matrix. Also untested: whether source / mirror survives across two gateway-joined clusters with different domains (row 5's topology), the hub as a real JetStream store rather than a pass-through, leaf reconnect behaviour with many regions, and mirror catch-up time at production volume.
