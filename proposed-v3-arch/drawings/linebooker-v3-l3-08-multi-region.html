Placement, Routing and the Border Contract

What decides where a tenant's data lives, how a request reaches the cell that is allowed to hold it, and what may cross a national border. This is a directional document: a first-pass position recorded so the platform can move, not a settled decision, stated as at 3 September 2026. Nothing on this sheet is built — the lab runs one server in one region — and no V3 decision of record exists yet. Messaging mechanics belong to LB-V3-L3-05; the placement record, allow-list text and country rules belong to L4.

SCOPE

One question. Where may a tenant's data live, and what may cross a border? Availability and recovery are on sheet 2.

DIRECTION

Directional. A first pass as at 2026-09-03 so work can start. Not a decision, and not compliance evidence.

PLACEMENT

A placement record names the cells one tenant may use. It belongs to the tenant account, not to a person or a browser.

CLASS

Every item carries a data class. The class, not the service, decides what happens at a border.

ROUTING

The tenant is resolved first, then an ingress is chosen. Where a person happens to be decides nothing.

LOCAL

The page, the app shell and the plugin registry are served from the same cell as the data they show.

MIRROR

Durable history crosses only as a named copy. A copy is preferred over a live link because it resumes after a break.

SOVEREIGN

A sovereign cell has no regulated path out, in any direction. Backups and support sessions are included in that.

ACCOUNT

A tenant is one account in every permitted cell. The account carries the tenant; the deployment carries the region.

OPEN

Every amber tile needs a named owner to confirm it. Ten open items are listed in the L3-08 register.

Availability, Recovery and Failure by Jurisdiction

What keeps one cell running, how that cell is backed up and restored without leaving its jurisdiction, what each data class is allowed to lose, and what the platform does when a region, a border link or the control plane is lost. This is a directional document: a first-pass position stated as at 3 September 2026, not a settled decision. Nothing on this sheet is built, no recovery target has been agreed, and no V3 decision of record exists. Sizing, schedules and procedures belong to L4.

SCOPE

One question. What keeps a cell up, how is it recovered in place, and what happens when a region is lost? Placement is on sheet 1.

DIRECTION

Directional. A first pass as at 2026-09-03. Every recovery number below is deliberately shown as unset.

HA

Three servers and a standby are availability minimums, not capacity estimates. Sizing is a later, separate job.

BACKUP

A backup is part of the data. It follows the same residency rule as the live copy, with no exception for convenience.

KEYS

A key held abroad puts the data abroad. Keys stay where the data stays, and so do the people who can use them.

TESTED

A restore that has never been tried is not a recovery plan. The rehearsal needs an owner and a date.

TARGETS

Nothing is agreed. Loss and restart windows per data class are the biggest open item on this sheet.

CELL LOST

A lost cell is restored in its own country. Whether a second site is funded is not decided.

CONTROL

If the control plane cannot be reached, a running cell keeps running. Only new claims and policy edits stop.

OPEN

Ten open items sit in the L3-08 register, including three inherited from the L3-05 gateway questions.
