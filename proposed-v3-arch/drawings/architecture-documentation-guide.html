Architecture documentation guide

How do I find the right architecture detail?

The Proposed V3 architecture is drawn at five levels. Start at the top and zoom in. Each level answers one kind of question for one kind of reader, and every document sits under exactly one parent.

Five levels — what each one tells you
LevelDocumentIt tells youWritten forYou will not find
L0AtlasWhere to find the document you need: the hierarchy, IDs, titles and status.anyonesystem design
L1OverviewWho the platform serves, the business capabilities, the external systems, and which country or region things run in.everybodyservice internals, protocols, schemas
L2ArchitectureHow the platform is structured, and which technologies are selected and why.architects, leads, opssubjects, payloads, permissions
L3Concern viewsHow one concern works — for example messaging, security, data or observability.specialists in that concernunrelated concerns
L4Detailed designsThe contracts, flows, schemas, permissions and deployment specifications needed to build one design.implementersthe full system landscape again

The levels branch. L2-01 has a sibling, L2-02 Technology Selection, and twelve L3 children such as Messaging / NATS, Data and Multi-Region. A detailed design develops one concern of its parent. A parent may still be PLANNED.

L4 is

L4 turns one architectural concern into an implementable design. Each document defines the relevant contracts, interactions, data structures, permissions or deployment details, and makes unresolved choices explicit.

ADRs
What was decided, and why

A separate log, not a level. One decision can govern any level.

Editions
One document, three forms

HTML drawing, PDF print, and optional prose page.

How far to trust what you read
PLANNED
Catalogued. Nothing to review yet.
DRAFT
Reviewable, but not accepted or not complete.
AVAILABLE
HTML and PDF match and passed validation.
SUPERSEDED
Kept for history. Links to its replacement.

Status is about the document being ready, not about every decision in it being approved. “Directional” on a sheet means a first-pass position, dated, with each provisional item marked and any missing ADR shown.
